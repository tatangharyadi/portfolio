# Most "AI transformation" efforts measure the wrong number

Most companies that stand up in an all-hands and announce an "AI transformation" are quietly
measuring the wrong number the whole time, and almost nobody in the room says so. The CFO
asks how many people logged in last quarter and nods at the number like it settled
something. Nobody in that room asks whether the work downstream of the login actually got
faster or cheaper. Those aren't the same question.

I get why the easy number wins. Usage pulls straight off a dashboard. Impact requires
someone to actually compare a specific process before the tool showed up and after, then
admit out loud in a meeting that the answer might be "not much." Whoever owns the initiative doesn't want to
run that comparison and hand a null result to their own VP.

So people show up with adoption rate and prompts run per week. You can't tell from either
number whether a single deploy actually shipped faster, or whether a single customer ticket
closed without a human ever touching it. All you know is that people opened the app. That's
it.

Here's the part that actually stings: the org usually already has the numbers to answer the
real question. Ticket resolution time. Deploy frequency. Somebody in engineering ops has
been tracking these for years. But nobody running the initiative bothers connecting it to
any of them, because that would mean committing in writing to a comparison that could come
back completely flat.

I've sat in the meeting where someone proposed exactly this in front of the whole leadership
team: pull the before/after cycle time and put it next to the adoption chart. I believed for
a while that once that comparison existed, leadership would want to see it. I was wrong.
Someone on the leadership team quietly dropped it from the deck before the next planning
cycle even started. Not because the number was bad. Nobody had run it yet. Asking the
question out loud just made the adoption chart look thin by comparison, and thin charts
don't survive contact with a VP.

If your initiative can't point to something like ticket resolution time or deploy frequency
moving after it started, you don't have evidence of impact. You have evidence that people
logged in.

Keep the effort. Just stop reporting the login count like it means something it doesn't.
